Lounge suit dress code, explained

A proper suit and tie.

By si187 Apps · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read

Lounge suit, sometimes written as business formal or simply formal, is common on UK wedding invitations, formal daytime events and some race days. Despite the name, it isn't relaxed: it means a matching suit with a tie.

What to wear, piece by piece

Jacket
A matching suit in navy, grey or charcoal. Two-piece is standard; a three-piece adds a little extra polish.
Shirt and tie
A pressed shirt in white, light blue or a subtle stripe, with a tie. The tip of the tie should land at the middle of your belt buckle.
Trousers
The matching suit trousers, hemmed to just touch the top of the shoe.
Shoes
Polished black Oxfords are the safest choice; dark brown Oxfords or Derbies work for daytime and country events.
Extras
A pocket square, dark socks that match your trousers, and a belt that matches your shoes. With a three-piece suit, wear braces instead: no belt under a waistcoat.

What to avoid

  • An odd jacket and trousers.
  • Trainers of any kind.
  • Leaving off the tie.

Two ways to wear it

Keep it classic

Navy two-piece suit, white shirt, burgundy tie and black Oxfords.

Push it a little

Mid-grey three-piece suit, pale pink shirt, navy knitted tie and dark brown suede shoes for a daytime wedding.

Quick questions

Does a black suit count as a lounge suit?

Technically yes, but navy or grey look better in daylight and are more versatile.

Can I take my tie off later?

At weddings, usually once the evening reception starts. Follow the groom's lead.

What does lounge suit mean at the races?

A suit and tie, often with a little more colour or pattern than at the office. Check the enclosure's own dress code too, as some are stricter.
